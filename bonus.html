How bonuses are taxed

A bonus is "supplemental wages". Many employers withhold federal income tax on it at a flat 22% (37% on the part of supplemental pay above $1 million in a year). Others add it to your regular paycheck and withhold using the "aggregate" method, which can give a different percentage. Social Security, Medicare and state tax also apply.

Withholding is not your final tax. Your bonus is added to your income for the year, so it is really taxed at your normal bracket rates when you file. This calculator shows both numbers, so you can see whether you may get money back or owe a little more. It assumes the flat 22% method and leaves out local taxes.

Common questions

Why does my bonus look taxed at 22%?

Employers often withhold federal income tax on bonuses at the flat 22% supplemental rate. That is withholding, not your final tax rate, and Social Security, Medicare and state tax come out as well.

Are bonuses really taxed more than regular pay?

No. At filing, your bonus is added to your income and taxed at your normal bracket rates. Withholding can be higher or lower than what you finally owe, so you may get money back or owe a little.

Can I lower tax on a bonus?

One common way is putting part of it into a pre-tax 401(k), if your plan allows it. Talk to your payroll team or a tax professional.
