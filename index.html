How this paycheck calculator works

We start with your gross pay, subtract any pre-tax 401(k) contribution, then apply the 2026 federal tax brackets after the standard deduction for your filing status. Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% on high earners) are taken from your gross wages. State income tax is estimated from the state you pick, or from a rate you type in.

This tool does not include local or city taxes, tax credits, other income, or extra W-4 withholding.

Common questions

Why is my real paycheck different from this estimate?

Your employer withholds based on your W-4, benefits such as health insurance, local taxes and state payroll taxes. This calculator uses the standard deduction, one job, no credits and an approximate state rate, so treat the result as a close estimate, not an exact figure.

What are Social Security and Medicare taxes?

They are the FICA payroll taxes. In 2026 Social Security is 6.2% of wages up to $184,500, and Medicare is 1.45% of all wages, plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 married filing jointly).

Which states have no income tax on wages?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax wages. For other states this calculator uses an approximate rate, and you can type your own.

Does this include a 401(k)?

Yes. Enter a percentage of pay in the pre-tax 401(k) box. It lowers your federal and state income tax, but not Social Security or Medicare tax.
