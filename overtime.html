How overtime pay works

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), most non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. For example, at $25 an hour and 48 hours in a week, you get 40 hours at $25 ($1,000) plus 8 hours at $37.50 ($300), which is $1,300.

Some states, such as California, also require overtime after 8 hours in a day or double time at certain points, so check your state's rules. Salaried employees can still be eligible for overtime unless they meet the exemption tests for duties and salary level.

Common questions

Who is entitled to overtime pay?

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, most hourly workers and many salaried workers are "non-exempt" and must get at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Salaried employees are exempt only if they pass salary-level and job-duty tests.

What is the "regular rate"?

It is more than base pay. It can include non-discretionary bonuses and shift differentials. This calculator uses the hourly rate you type in.

Do state overtime rules differ?

Yes. Some states, such as California, require overtime after 8 hours in a day, and double time at certain points. This calculator uses a weekly threshold only, so check your state's rules.
