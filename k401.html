How the 401(k) calculator works

For 2026 you can put up to $24,500 of your pay into a 401(k). If you are 50 or older you can add $8,000 more, and ages 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead. Traditional 401(k) contributions come out before income tax, so they lower your tax bill this year.

The projection adds your contributions and employer match at the end of each year and assumes a steady return with no pay raises.

Common questions

What is the 2026 401(k) contribution limit?

You can put in up to $24,500 of your own pay. If you are 50 or older you can add $8,000, and ages 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead. Employer contributions are on top of that, under a higher total limit.

Do employer matches count toward the $24,500?

No. The employee limit applies to your own deferrals. Employer matching counts toward the overall annual additions limit, which is higher.

Traditional or Roth 401(k)?

A traditional 401(k) lowers your taxable income today and is taxed when you withdraw. A Roth is taxed now and can be tax-free later. This calculator models the traditional type.
